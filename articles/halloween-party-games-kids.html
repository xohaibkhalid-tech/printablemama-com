10 Halloween Party Games for Kids (That Don't Cost a Thing)

Here is a truth nobody tells you when you become a parent: Halloween parties are the easiest parties you will ever throw. The theme does all the work. You hang a few paper bats, put on a playlist of spooky sound effects, and suddenly your living room is a haunted house and you are a party-planning genius. The bar is a pumpkin. Everything else is bonus.

But the games — the games are where Halloween parties live or die. Get them right and you have twenty minutes of joyful chaos per game. Get them wrong and you have small sugar-fuelled gremlins running in circles while you quietly recalculate your life choices. Below are ten games that actually work, most of them free, all of them tested by the toughest critics in the business: other people's children.

Why Halloween games are secretly the easiest games

Every other party theme makes you invent the fun from scratch. A dinosaur party? You are Googling “dinosaur games for 6 year olds” at midnight. Halloween just hands you the fun. Costumes, candy, things that glow in the dark — the ingredients are already in your house. Your job is not to create excitement; it is to aim the excitement in a direction that does not involve the curtains.

The other advantage: Halloween games run themselves on a timer that nature provides. Kids arrive already at an 8 out of 10. You want games with clear winners, short rounds, and an ending — because an open-ended Halloween game does not end, it just gets louder.

The 10 games

1. The Halloween scavenger hunt

This is my number-one pick, and not just because it is ours. A scavenger hunt turns the whole house — or the whole block — into the game board. Our free Halloween scavenger hunt has twelve spooky things for kids to find, from jack-o'-lanterns to black cats to something that glows in the dark. Print one per kid, hand out pencils, set a twenty-minute timer. Put the under-6s in teams of two or three with a grown-up. First team to tick off all twelve picks the first candy bowl. House rule. Non-negotiable.

2. Wrap the mummy

The classic for a reason. Split the kids into teams of three: one mummy, two wrappers, one roll of toilet paper per team. Three minutes on the clock, and the best mummy wins. The unwrapping is half the fun — and yes, you will be finding toilet paper behind the sofa until Christmas. Budget one extra roll for the kid who “wraps” themselves. It happens every year.

3. Pin the hat on the witch

The Halloween version of pin the tail on the donkey, and honestly better, because the witch looks fabulous with a hat stuck on her nose. Draw a big witch on a sheet of paper (or let the kids draw her — wobbly witches are the best witches), cut out paper hats, add a sticker or tape dot to each. Blindfold, spin once, and let chaos reign. For kids under five, skip the blindfold and just let them stick — they will have just as much fun and nobody gets poked.

4. Halloween bingo

Bingo is the undercover genius of party games: it looks calm, but the kids are absolutely wired. Call out pumpkins, ghosts, bats and candy while they hunt for the squares. Our free Halloween bingo cards are ready to print, and if you want to make your own custom cards with the kids' names or inside jokes, the bingo card generator does it in seconds. Small candy prizes for a full row keep the stakes appropriately high.

5. Pumpkin ring toss

Take three or four pumpkins of different sizes, line them up, and toss glow bracelets or rings cut from paper plates over the stems. The smallest pumpkin is worth the most points, obviously. This game has a beautiful property: it is just hard enough that kids keep trying “three more goes, I nearly had it!” and just easy enough that someone actually wins within five minutes.

6. Ghost bowling

Empty plastic bottles, a white sheet or white bin bags, a marker for ghost faces — you have bowling pins. Set them up, roll a small pumpkin or an orange ball down the hallway, and watch the ghosts fly. Ten pins is the full setup, but six is plenty for a party. Pro tip from hard experience: put the pins on carpet, not tile. Tile makes ghosts slide into the kitchen, and the kitchen is where the cake is.

7. The colouring station (your secret weapon)

Every Halloween party needs one calm game, and this is it. Set up a table with our free Halloween colouring pages and a cup of crayons, and you have bought yourself the most valuable party currency there is: a wind-down activity. Kids drift to the colouring table between games, after cake, and during that dangerous stretch when the party is technically over but nobody has left. It is not the loudest game. It is the one you will be most grateful for. Pair it with our Halloween colouring ideas if you want to theme it up.

8. The trick-or-treat trail

If you have a garden or a hallway, you have a trail. Hide wrapped sweets or small toys along a short route, give each kid a paper bag, and send them off in pairs with a grown-up at the back of the line. For older kids, add riddles at each stop (“I have a face but no body, I glow when it is dark” — you know the one). It is trick-or-treating without the logistics of the whole neighbourhood.

9. Monster dance freeze

Play Halloween music — the Monster Mash is legally required — and everyone dances like their best monster: zombie shuffle, vampire glide, witch cackle-dance. When the music stops, freeze. Anyone who wobbles is out (or, for the little ones, does a silly penalty like three monster jumps and rejoins). Last monster standing wins. This is the game that burns the most sugar-energy per minute, which is exactly what you want at 4pm.

10. Guess how many candies

Fill a jar with candy corn or wrapped sweets, and let each kid write down their guess with their name. Closest guess takes the jar home. It takes five minutes to set up, runs itself all party long, and gives you a built-in conversation piece on the snack table. Announce the winner right before cake — it is the perfect hype moment, and the winner gets a jar of sugar on top of the cake sugar, which is frankly a bold move.

How to time the whole thing

For a two-hour party, you only need four or five games. Start with the scavenger hunt or the trick-or-treat trail while everyone arrives and the energy is wild. Then hit the active games — mummy wrap, dance freeze, ghost bowling — before cake. After cake, and this matters, go calm: bingo, then the colouring station. Sugar plus sitting-down games is how you get everyone home in one piece. Finish with the candy-jar guess so every kid leaves a winner at something.

The wind-down game that saves your evening

Here is the part nobody plans and everybody needs. The last twenty minutes of a Halloween party are chaos in costume: tired kids, wired kids, parents arriving at slightly different times, goodie bags to hand out. This is when you deploy the colouring station. Print a stack of our Halloween colouring pages, put them on the table with the crayons, and let the kids colour while they wait for pickup. It looks adorable, it is silent enough to hear yourself think, and it sends every child home with something they made.

The bottom line

A Halloween party does not need a rented venue, a themed cake that costs more than your car service, or games ordered from the internet. It needs kids in costumes, a few games with clear endings, and one calm activity for the comedown. Start with the scavenger hunt, end with the colouring table, and in between just aim the excitement. You have got this — and the paper bats on the wall will make you look like you planned it for weeks.
