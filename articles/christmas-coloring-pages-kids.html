10 Jolly Christmas Coloring Ideas for Kids (Free Printables Inside)

There's a specific sound that December makes in a house with small children, and it's not sleigh bells. It's the sound of someone very small asking "is it Christmas yet?" for the forty-seventh time before 9am — usually while standing on the good sofa in their socks.

Christmas with kids is wonderful and completely exhausting in equal measure, because for them the whole month is one long waiting room. The presents are wrapped and sitting right there. The advent calendar chocolate is somehow already gone by the 3rd. And time, for a four-year-old in December, moves at roughly the speed of a glacier.

This is where coloring pages stop being a "nice little activity" and become a genuine parenting tool. Not the fancy kind — just crayons, paper, and fifteen minutes where nobody is asking you anything. Here are ten Christmas coloring ideas that'll carry you from the first of December right through to the big day, plus a free printable with six of the best ready to print tonight.

Why Christmas coloring actually works

Here's the honest truth: most December activities for kids are either expensive, messy, or require strapping everyone into the car. Coloring is none of those things. It costs pennies, the mess stays on the table (mostly), and it happens at home while the kettle boils.

The subject matter helps too. Christmas things are genuinely fun to color — big simple shapes, and kids already have fierce opinions about what color everything should be. The tree is green, obviously. Unless your five-year-old has decided it's purple this year, in which case the tree is purple and you'd be wise not to argue.

The 10 jolliest Christmas coloring ideas

1. The Christmas tree (the big one)

Every Christmas coloring collection starts here, and for good reason — a tree is basically three triangles stacked up, which means even a two-year-old can have a proper go at it. The fun is in the decorations: each ornament is a tiny circle begging for a different color, and the star on top is non-negotiable. Our free Christmas coloring pages lead with exactly this tree, baubles and all.

2. Santa himself

The beard is the whole event here. Kids take the beard VERY seriously — it must be white, it must be fluffy, and woe betide anyone who suggests otherwise. (The suit, on the other hand, is apparently open to interpretation. We've seen green Santas. We've seen a blue one.) The face on our printable has big simple features, so little ones can manage the eyes and nose without everything drifting off-center.

3. A reindeer with proper antlers

Antlers are nature's coloring-book gift: branching lines that are basically impossible to get wrong. Every tine is another little line to trace, which makes reindeer weirdly good for practicing controlled strokes. And yes, the nose gets colored red. It is the law. Our printable's reindeer even has a collar with a bell, for the detail-oriented child who will absolutely notice if you skip it.

4. A snowman in a top hat

Three circles, a carrot nose, done — the snowman is the confidence-builder of Christmas coloring. What makes it fun is the accessories: the top hat, the scarf, the twig arms. Each one is a separate little coloring decision, which stretches a simple page into a proper ten-minute sit-down. Tip from experience: let them give the snowman a silly face. The wonky smile is always better than the neat one.

5. A stocking with something poking out

There's something about a stocking that fires up a kid's imagination — because the whole point of a stocking is what's INSIDE it. Our printable has a candy cane poking out of the cuff, plus a snowflake on the leg for good measure. This is also the page that starts conversations: "what would YOU put in your stocking?" (Budget for the answers to be ambitious.)

6. The gingerbread man

I'm going to be honest with you: the gingerbread man gets colored first. Every time. There's something about the little guy — the icing squiggles, the bow tie, the buttons — that kids find irresistible. He's also the perfect size for cutting out afterwards, which brings us to...

7. Turn them into gift tags

Here's the upgrade that makes you look like a Pinterest mom with about six minutes of effort: color the pages, cut the designs out along the dashed lines, punch a hole in the corner, thread some ribbon through. Done — handmade gift tags. A present with a tag your kid colored themselves beats a store-bought tag every single time, and grandparents in particular will lose their minds over them. Pair with our Christmas gift list so you actually remember who got what.

8. Handmade Christmas cards

Same principle, bigger canvas. Fold a piece of card in half, glue a colored design on the front, and let the kids scribble (or dictate) a message inside. These are the cards that get kept — nobody keeps a store-bought card, but a lopsided Santa glued to folded card? That's on the mantelpiece until February. Our printable Christmas card gives you a proper template if you want one.

9. A December countdown chain

Print two copies of the page, cut out all twelve designs, and tape them into a paper chain — one link per day until Christmas. The kids color them first, then each morning they tear one link off. It's a visual countdown that makes the waiting bearable, and by Christmas Day the chain is gone and the excitement is at maximum. Way cheaper than a toy advent calendar, and no chocolate arguments.

10. The window gallery

Tape the finished pages to the front window, facing OUT. Your street gets free Christmas decorations, the neighbors get a smile on the school run, and your kid gets an audience for their art. The snowman and the tree look particularly good backlit. Just use the painter's tape, not the heavy-duty stuff — learn from my mistakes.

How to print them so they actually look good

The boring-but-important printer notes, because nothing kills coloring enthusiasm like a page that comes out grey and smeary:

  • Plain white paper, 100% scale. Don't let the printer "fit to page" — it shrinks everything. These pages are built for one clean sheet.
  • Black and white is the point. They're line-art pages; color printing just wastes ink on outlines that should be black. Draft or grayscale mode is completely fine.
  • Print a stack, not one. Kids go through coloring pages the way teenagers go through snacks. Six copies cost almost nothing and save a reprint trip mid-afternoon.
  • Crayons beat markers for little ones. Markers bleed through and end up on the table, the dog, and somehow the ceiling. Washable crayons keep the mess contained. Save the markers for the 6+ crowd with a spare sheet underneath.

What to do with the mountain of finished pages

By mid-December you will own approximately four hundred colored Christmas pages. The fridge only holds so many. Beyond the gift tags and cards above: string a few into a garland across the mantel, use the tree page as a festive placemat on Christmas Eve, or start a "Christmas art folder" — one per year, dated. Future you will be so glad you did. And when the crayons finally run dry, the full coloring collection has dinosaurs, ocean animals, unicorns and space scenes for the rest of the year — plus our Halloween pages and Thanksgiving gratitude printable from earlier this season if you missed them.

The bottom line

December is a marathon disguised as a holiday. The magic is real — the lights, the music, the look on their faces Christmas morning — but so is the tiredness, and there's no prize for doing it all on zero quiet minutes. Print the pages, hand over the crayons, and take the fifteen minutes. Wrap a present. Drink the tea while it's hot. You've earned it, and January-you says thanks.
