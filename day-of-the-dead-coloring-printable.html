Day of the Dead Coloring Pages — 6 Designs for Kids

Every year, right after the Halloween candy buckets come home, a quieter celebration starts: Día de los Muertos, the Day of the Dead, on November 1 and 2. If your kids have never heard of it, they are about to ask you a lot of questions — and honestly, that is the best part.

It is a Mexican tradition where families remember the people they loved who have died. Not in a sad, hushed way, but with marigolds everywhere, candles glowing, photos on a little altar called an ofrenda, and the favourite foods of the people being remembered. Kids get it faster than grown-ups do: we set a place for grandma, we tell her stories, we remember her out loud.

These six coloring designs are the gentle way in. A sugar skull to decorate however they like, a big marigold, a glowing candle, bright papel picado banners, a guitar for the music, and a small ofrenda with a photo frame and flowers. Thick lines, big shapes, one page. Print it, hand over the crayons, and let the questions come when they come.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are these Day of the Dead coloring pages really free?

Yes — every design is free for personal and classroom use. Print one for the fridge or a whole stack for the class. No sign-up, no watermark, no catch.

How do I print the Day of the Dead coloring pages?

Hit the Print button above. They come out best on plain white paper at 100% scale — one page of black-and-white line art, so any printer will do the job.

What ages are they for?

The outlines are thick and the shapes are big, which suits ages 2 to 8 best. Older kids like them too, especially the sugar skull, which they can decorate with as much detail as they want.

What is Día de los Muertos?

Día de los Muertos, or Day of the Dead, is a Mexican tradition held on November 1 and 2. Families remember loved ones who have died, with marigolds, candles, photos and their favourite foods. It is a celebration of memory, not a scary holiday.

Can teachers use them in the classroom?

Yes — print a stack for the week of November 1. They pair nicely with a short lesson about how different cultures remember the people they love.
