Typing Practice Sheets

A free printable typing kit for students — finger placement chart, three timed paragraphs and a progress log. Print it, practice a little each day, and watch your speed climb.

Here's something nobody tells students early enough: typing speed quietly decides how much of your homework actually gets done. A student who types 20 words a minute spends twice as long on every essay, every report, every online assignment as the one who types 40. It's not about talent — it's about practice, and practice works best when it's short, daily, and you can see your progress.

That's exactly what this kit is for. The finger chart teaches your hands where to live on the keyboard. The timed paragraphs give you real text to race against the clock. And the log sheet turns invisible progress into numbers you can watch improve week after week. Ten minutes a day is plenty. Print the kit, grab a timer, and start.

1. Finger Placement Chart

Print & keep beside your keyboard

Rest your fingers on the home row — A S D F for the left hand, J K L ; for the right. Each color shows which finger presses which keys. F and J have small bumps so you can find home without looking.

Left pinky Left ring Left middle Left index Right index Right middle Right ring Right pinky Thumbs (space bar)

Rule of thumb: never look at your fingers while typing. Glance at the chart instead — your hands learn faster when your eyes stay on the screen.

2. Timed Practice Paragraphs

3 minutes each

Type each paragraph without looking at your fingers. Count your words when the timer ends — that's your words-per-minute (WPM).

3:00 A soft beep marks the end.

Beginner — home row only

Difficulty: ★☆☆Focus: A S D F J K L ;
A sad lad had a glass of ale. Dad asked Sal to add a salad. Sal had a fad for fall salads. All fall, Sal gladly ladled salad. A flask fell; a lad fled. Dad laughed as Sal gasped. A lass asked Dad for a small salad. Dad had all salads half off. Sal had a gala salad day.

Intermediate — full keyboard

Difficulty: ★★☆Focus: reach keys & rhythm
The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog near the river bank. My neighbor's quiet cat watched every move with bright green eyes. Pack your bags and bring warm jackets, because the mountain trip begins at dawn. Wendy kept six dozen jars of pickles on the pantry shelf. A strong wind blew the kite over the tall pine trees.

Advanced — punctuation & capitals

Difficulty: ★★★Focus: Shift, commas, capitals
Dear Ms. Rivera: Thank you for your letter of March 4th! Our team — all twelve of us — will visit your library on Friday, May 16, at 10:30 a.m. Please confirm the meeting room (Building C, 2nd floor) by Wednesday. We plan to discuss budgets, schedules, and the new reading program. Sincerely, Mr. Thomas J. Blake, Principal.

How to score: count the words you finished, divide by 3 for WPM. Count your mistakes; accuracy = (correct words ÷ total words) × 100. Write both in your log below.
